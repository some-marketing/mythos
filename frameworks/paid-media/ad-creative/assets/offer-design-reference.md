# Offer Design (reference for ad creative)

Provenance: `_dev/research/external-skills/marketingskills/distilled_from.json`. Use as background judgment when writing ad creative — the creative *expresses* an offer; this note is about whether the offer underneath is worth expressing.

## The one idea that matters for ad creative

**The offer is the thing, not the ad.** Better copy on a weak offer compounds slowly; a stronger offer with average copy converts immediately. When ad creative under-performs and the targeting/placement are sound, suspect the *offer* before rewriting the hook for the fifth time. Flag this back to the campaign owner rather than burning creative iterations on a fixed-but-weak offer.

## The value equation (diagnostic lens before writing a headline)

```
            Dream Outcome  x  Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
  Value  = ----------------------------------------------------------
            Time Delay     x   Effort & Sacrifice
```

Four levers; the lowest-scoring one is the binding constraint and is what the ad should lead with:

| Lever | Move it by | Creative implication |
|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome ↑ | name the bigger goal behind the surface ask | lead the hook with the outcome, not the feature |
| Perceived likelihood ↑ | proof: named customers, data, guarantees, specific method | put the proof element in the ad (real number, real name) |
| Time delay ↓ | faster first win / shorter timeline | state the speed concretely ("same day", "in two weeks") |
| Effort & sacrifice ↓ | done-for-you, fewer decisions, lower learning curve | promise the low-effort path explicitly |

"Lower the price" requests are usually "raise the numerator / lower the denominator" requests. Price is the *comparison*, not the value — so the ad rarely needs to lead with discount.

## The six offer components (what an ad can surface)

Core deliverable · bonus stack · guarantee / risk reversal · scarcity-or-urgency · name · price + payment structure. Most weak offers fail on bonuses (none), guarantee (none or wrong type), or scarcity (none, or fake). An ad usually surfaces 1–2 of these — pick the one that moves the binding lever.

## Honesty guardrails (align with Mythos attribution + slop linting)

These are hard "do not" lines that map directly onto our existing creative gates:

- **No fake scarcity** — fake countdown timers, "only 3 spots left" lies. Short-term lift, long-term trust collapse. (Reinforces `lint-attributions` / no-fabrication discipline.)
- **No over-promising guarantees** — "double your revenue or refund + $1,000" nukes reputation on the failures.
- **No bonus inflation** — "$50K of bonuses" on a $497 product; sophisticated buyers see through it.
- **Banned vocabulary in creative:** "game-changing / revolutionary / disruptive / next-level / 10x", "secret / what they don't want you to know", "limited time" with no real limit, "worth $X" with no comparable, "100% guaranteed" without conditions. Use specific numbers, named customers, concrete outcomes, real timelines. Specificity beats superlatives — same rule as our voice-archetypes and slop-pattern bank.

## When this does NOT apply

Pure self-serve SaaS with tiered subscriptions: the levers are tier structure / value metric / packaging, not bonus-and-guarantee construction. For that, route to pricing/packaging thinking instead.
