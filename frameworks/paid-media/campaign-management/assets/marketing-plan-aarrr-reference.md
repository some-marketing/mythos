## Why this lives here

Campaign management decides spend, structure, and optimization for paid media. Those decisions are stronger when tagged to a funnel stage and anchored to a defensible budget. This note pulls the two reusable lenses from the external `marketing-plan` skill: **AARRR stage-tagging** and **scientific budget setting**. The full 13-section fCMO plan is out of scope for this framework — route whole-funnel planning elsewhere.

## AARRR — tag every campaign to a stage

Forcing each campaign to a funnel stage makes the media plan executable in priority order:

- **Acquisition** — strangers → aware (top of funnel; most prospecting/broad paid sits here)
- **Activation** — aware → first valued experience (signup, onboarding, first session — usually retargeting/lead campaigns)
- **Retention** — repeat users (lifecycle; rarely paid, but win-back retargeting can sit here)
- **Referral** — retained users bring more users (programs, not usually paid spend)
- **Revenue** — monetization: pricing, upsells, bundles, ACV expansion

Brand and content are **cross-cutting**, not a stage. Practical use: before launching a campaign, name its AARRR stage. If two live campaigns claim the same stage with the same audience, that is a structural overlap to resolve, not two campaigns to optimize separately.

## Setting a paid-media budget defensibly

Two methods (use whichever the available data supports), then add a 10–20% experimental layer on top:

1. **Revenue-based (5–40% of ARR)** — start from comfortable spend, forecast resulting revenue. Best when historical CAC data exists.
2. **Goal-based** — reverse-engineer from the revenue target:
   `budget = [ (New ARR / (ARPC x 12)) x CAC ] / annual_retention_rate`
   Best for a fixed goal or fundraising context.

The **experimental 10–20%** funds the next-channel test before the current channel plateaus. CAC is the main dependency — if CAC is unknown, the first budget is a *test budget* to discover it, not a scaled budget.

## Funding/maturity-stage spend anchors (ballpark, adjust by category)

Useful when a client asks "how much should we be spending on ads": consumer/ecom can spend more, deep-tech B2B less.

| Stage | Monthly paid ballpark |
|---|---|
| Bootstrapped / pre-seed | $0–$2K (organic-first; little/no paid) |
| Seed close | $5–$15K paid test budget |
| Seed deployment | $20–$50K paid |
| Series A | $50–$150K paid (performance + content) |
| Series B+ | $150K+ (brand campaigns added) |

These are anchors for sizing a defensible test budget, not targets to hit. Always confirm the actual number against the client's CAC and stated revenue goal.
